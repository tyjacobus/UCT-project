Why Quantum Complexity?

Before we write down a single qubit, let’s explain why a complexity theorist should care about quantum computation, and how it fits into the landscape of classes we have been building in CS455.

Guiding Question

Is $\mathsf{BPP}$ really the right notion of “efficiently computable”?

The thesis that CS455 quietly relies on

Recall from the Introduction to CS455 that the standard models of computation simulate each other with at most polynomial slowdown, which is why $\mathsf{P}$ and $\mathsf{BPP}$ are robust. The belief that this holds for every physically reasonable model has a name:

Extended Church–Turing Thesis. Any physically reasonable model of computation can be simulated by a probabilistic TM with at most polynomial overhead in time.

Randomness was the first serious candidate for a resource that might break the deterministic version of this thesis, and we handled it by defining $\mathsf{BPP}$ and asking whether $\mathsf{P}=\mathsf{BPP}$. Quantum mechanics is the most serious challenge to the probabilistic version.

Quantum mechanics challenges the thesis

In 1982 Feynman observed that simulating a quantum system of $m$ particles on a classical computer seems to require keeping track of about $2^m$ numbers (we will see why in the Qubits and Quantum States chapter), and asked whether we could harness this. In 1993 Bernstein and Vazirani defined the class $\mathsf{BQP}$ (bounded-error quantum polynomial time), the quantum analog of $\mathsf{BPP}$. The evidence that it is genuinely more powerful is strong:

Fact (Evidence for and against quantum power)
  1. Shor (1994). Factoring integers and computing discrete logarithms can be done in quantum polynomial time. Factoring is not known to be in $\mathsf{BPP}$ (the best known classical algorithms take time roughly $2^{\tilde O(n^{1/3})}$), and the security of RSA rests on it being hard.
  2. Simon (1994). There is an oracle $A$ with $\mathsf{BPP}^A\ne\mathsf{BQP}^A$: a quantum algorithm makes $O(n)$ queries where every randomized algorithm needs $\Omega(2^{n/2})$. Raz and Tal (2018) even give an oracle with $\mathsf{BQP}^A\not\subseteq\mathsf{PH}^A$.
  3. Grover (1996) and Bennett–Bernstein–Brassard–Vazirani (1997). Unstructured search over $N$ items takes $\Theta(\sqrt N)$ quantum queries, only a quadratic speed-up. Relative to a random oracle, $\mathsf{NP}\not\subseteq\mathsf{BQP}$. This is evidence that quantum computers do not solve $\mathsf{NP}$-complete problems efficiently.
Remark (Omitted details)

We do not prove these results. As with the oracle results in the Relativization chapter, the oracle separations are evidence, not proofs. For Shor’s and Simon’s algorithms see Arora–Barak, Chapter 10, and Nielsen–Chuang, Chapter 5; for Grover and the matching lower bound see Nielsen–Chuang, Chapter 6. The original papers are listed under References.

So if large quantum computers can be built and factoring is not in $\mathsf{BPP}$, the Extended Church–Turing Thesis is false. Note the third item, though: quantum computers do not work by “trying all answers in parallel”. A quantum state involves exponentially many classical strings at once, but a measurement shows you just one of them, at random. The real resource is subtler: quantum algorithms arrange for the “wrong” computation paths to cancel each other out. These notes are largely about making that sentence precise.

What we will prove

As with every new class in CS455, we pin $\mathsf{BQP}$ down by proving inclusions from both sides:

  • From below: $\mathsf{P}\subseteq\mathsf{BPP}\subseteq\mathsf{BQP}$ (the BQP and Reversible Computation chapter). This is not obvious, because quantum operations must be reversible.
  • From above: $\mathsf{BQP}\subseteq\mathsf{PSPACE}$ (the BQP ⊆ PSPACE chapter), and the sharper $\mathsf{BQP}\subseteq\mathsf{PP}$ (the Main Theorem chapter): a quantum computer can be simulated by a classical machine that can count.

The upper bound also explains why nobody has proved that quantum computers beat classical ones: by the Main Theorem, such a proof would show $\mathsf{P}\neq\mathsf{PP}$, and hence $\mathsf{P}\ne\mathsf{PSPACE}$ (see the Consequences Corollary). To keep the focus on complexity theory, we treat the rules of quantum mechanics as axioms (no physics is needed), and we omit quantum algorithms such as Shor’s.

Summary

  • “$\mathsf{BPP}$ = efficient” depends on the Extended Church–Turing Thesis, and quantum computing is the most serious known challenge to it.
  • $\mathsf{BQP}$ is to quantum circuits what $\mathsf{BPP}$ is to PTMs. Our goal is the sandwich $\mathsf{BPP}\subseteq\mathsf{BQP}\subseteq\mathsf{PP}\subseteq\mathsf{PSPACE}$.
  • The power of quantum computing comes from cancellation (interference), not from “parallel search”.
